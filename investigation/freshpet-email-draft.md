# Cover email — draft

**Subject:** Invoice #172825 — you were right, and here is what we found

---

Hi [Name],

I owe you a straight answer rather than a defence.

I came into this wanting to talk about how we could work together to improve the PM
program. Then I read your review properly and went and looked for myself — and what I found
was worse than what you flagged. So let me start there instead.

You were right on all three counts. The duplicated serial plates, the completed inspection
forms for stores that were closed, the missing plate photographs and reused equipment
images — every example you listed reproduced when we checked it, and when we widened the
audit beyond your examples we found more of the same. We did not stop at invoice #172825.
We went back through every PM report our app has produced since we started using it in
June — 592 reports — because it would have been dishonest to correct only the ones you
happened to catch.

The cause was our software, not your review being unfair. The app we piloted this summer let
photographs be attached from a phone's photo gallery instead of requiring the camera, and it
pre-filled inspection answers on the form. Combined with paperwork being completed in a
batch at the end of a route rather than at each store, that produced exactly the pattern you
identified. I am not going to defend documents that misrepresent what happened. I am telling
you how they came to exist, because you should know whether you are dealing with a broken
process or something worse. It was a broken process, and it was ours.

The attached document answers each of your three items in detail. The short version:

- **Duplicate serials** — we traced 144 plate photographs back to the units they actually
  show and reattached them correctly, and removed 76 photographs from the 73 reports where
  they documented somebody else's equipment. Every serial you listed now appears on exactly
  one report. Nothing was deleted; every original submission is preserved and every
  corrected report carries a stamp saying what changed.
- **Closed stores and missing units** — all five are withdrawn as billable work. The visits
  were real and we have the storefront photographs, but the completed checklists came from
  our form's defaults, not from the technician, and I am not going to ask you to accept
  them. The app now records a site visit exception properly, and those can never be
  invoiced.
- **Missing plate photographs** — after recovering everything recoverable, 196 reports
  still have no photograph of their own unit's plate. **We are re-visiting every one of
  them at our cost** and will supply the new documentation as it comes in. We are not
  asking you to pay for work we cannot evidence.

The defects are fixed and live: camera-only photographs, a serial-plate photograph required
before a report can be submitted, an automatic check that refuses any image already used on
another report, no pre-filled answers, and a proper exception path for closed stores.

One thing I do want to raise, and only because it affects the equipment rather than the
invoice: in several places we found the opposite problem — more units on site than the asset
list carries. At two stores we have a paper checklist signed by your store's own staff
confirming two units where the list shows one. I am not billing for those and I am not
asking you to take our word for it. But if a unit is not on the list, it is not being
maintained, and I would rather reconcile that with you than leave it.

I would still like to have the conversation about making this program better. I just did not
want to have it while this was sitting unaddressed, and I would rather you heard the full
extent of it from me than found the rest of it yourself.

Happy to walk through any report you want to look at, or to get on a call this week.

Sky Pace
Alameda Point Beverage Group
